Pledger

Your money, one movement at a time. Here is everything you need to know to use it.

  1. ✍️ Record every time money moves
  2. 🧮 Pledger adds it up into balances, gains and losses
  3. 📈 Watch your wealth change over time

Pledger is a personal ledger: a list of every time money moved, where it came from and where it went. From that list it works out how much you have in each account, how much you earned and spent, and how your wealth changes over time.

Each time money moves you add one movement: a date, what kind of movement it was, the two accounts involved and the amount. Everything else in Pledger is calculated from those movements.

💱 All amounts are in your base currency ({{ settings.currency }}). If you hold other currencies, enter their value converted to the base currency.

👁️ Showing Pledger to someone? The eye button at the top right replaces every amount with ****** until you click it again.

📋 Ledger
Your movements, newest first, with the form to add new ones. The cards at the top show your gains, losses and profit. Use the Month and Account filters to narrow everything down. The movements setting each account's initial amount are left out unless you tick Initial movements. Movements handled in another tab can't be deleted here: instead of ×, they show a colored circle linking to that tab: 🟢 Investments, 🔵 Currencies (conversions and revaluations), 🟠 Work (salaries), 🟣 Accounts (initial amounts) and 🟡 Debt (debt and loan movements, changed in the ledger CSV).
🏦 Accounts
Every account with its description and balance, your total assets, liabilities and equity, and the form to add a new account. With a month picked, balances show where each account stood at the end of that month. It also shows what your assets and liabilities are made of, by currency or by liquidity, and, at each month end, your debt over equity and your net liquidity (liquid assets minus liquid liabilities) over equity.
💱 Currencies
Your currencies: the inflation month by month of the base currency and the price index built from it, day by day (the months that haven't ended yet are projected), and, for each other currency, what it is worth in the base one over time next to its real value (that value with inflation taken out, in today's prices: if it goes down, the currency grew less than prices). It also shows the balances of the accounts in each currency, what the accounts in other currencies hold in their own currency, and how your assets are split by currency at each month end. Buying or selling another currency is a conversion: add it here, with the amount in each currency. Every time the currency's value changes, its accounts get a USD - Result gain or loss, so their balance always matches what they hold at that value.
📊 Results
Your gains and losses grouped by result type (click a result type to see what is inside it), a waterfall of the gains and then the losses of each result type adding up to your profit (with its table one click away), and a line showing your equity over time. Hover over the line to see each day. Initial movements are left out of the results here too, unless you tick Initial movements. Below them are your result types and base movements; you can add new ones here.
💼 Work
Your work history, month by month: the company and job position, the gross and net salary, and the date you received the payment. Months without a job are kept too, so no month is ever missing. Add the next month with the form, or click ✎ on a month to edit it. Two charts show your gross and net salary over time. Each month's gross salary also goes into the ledger week by week, split equally between the weeks of the month (Monday to Sunday, each in the month with at least 4 of its days). The Ledger tab shows these with a 🟠: change them by editing the month here.
📈 Investments
What you hold in your investment accounts: each investment with its type, quantity, amount and price (amount over quantity), and what your investments are made of, by type or by account. The investment results (capital gains and losses, interest, dividends, broker fees and stock splits) and the buying and selling of investments are added and deleted here, not in the Ledger tab, which shows them with a 🟢. Each one names its investment and, when buying, selling or splitting, its quantity, so the amounts and quantities above stay up to date. The interest on money earned or paid in a regular account (picking an Interest on Money base movement, with no investment) gets a section of its own, by account and month by month, and counts towards the total result. Two charts show the total invested and the total result month by month.
🤝 Debt
Who is in your debt accounts, split into people, services and companies: what you owe each of them, what they owe you, and your net debt. A movement of a debt account counts for whoever is named in its comment; the companies you worked for (from the Work tab) are listed automatically. A warning shows up when a movement names no one, or when someone owes the other way around from what is expected (by default, you owe services and companies owe you). The movements of the debt accounts are listed too, with who each one names.
💳 Credit Cards
What you owe on each credit card, plus what was charged to it and paid off it. With a month picked, the balance is the one at the end of that month, and the charges and payments are the ones of that month. A bar chart shows what was charged each month (your purchases, not your payments), and another one shows what you owe on them as a share of your equity, month by month, and their movements are listed below.

🏦 Accounts

An account is any place where money is kept or owed. There are three kinds:

  • Assets: what you own, such as cash, a bank account or investments.
  • Liabilities: what you owe, such as a credit card balance or a debt.
  • Equity: your net worth, that is, assets minus liabilities. There is a single equity account, and Pledger uses it on its own for gains and losses.

⚖️ Debit and credit

Every movement has two sides, and the amount is the same on both:

  • The debit account is where the money goes to ➡️.
  • The credit account is where the money comes from ⬅️.

For a liability this works the other way round from what you might expect: paying off a credit card debits the card, because you now owe less, and buying with the card credits it, because you now owe more.

🔀 Movement types

  • G Gain: money that makes you richer, such as a salary or a gift. It is credited to equity.
  • L Loss: money that makes you poorer, such as food, rent or fees. It is debited from equity.
  • T Transfer: money that moves between your own accounts, such as a cash withdrawal or a credit card payment. It does not change your equity.

🏷️ Base movements

A base movement is a category for movements, such as "Basic - Food" or "Work - Salary Main". Choosing one tells Pledger whether the movement is a gain, a loss or a transfer. Some base movements always use the same account on one side: in the form that side is filled in for you and cannot be changed. A side shown as - means any account can be used there.

🗂️ Result types

Result types group gains and losses into larger families, such as Operative (everyday income and spending), Investments or Taxes. Every gain and loss base movement belongs to exactly one result type; transfers have none. Result types are numbered, and the Results tab lists them in that order, with a running total that shows how each family adds to or takes from your profit.

✍️ Record a movement

  1. Go to Ledger.
  2. Pick the date and the type: gain, loss or transfer.
  3. Pick the base movement that best describes it.
  4. Choose the debit and credit accounts, unless they are already filled in.
  5. Enter the amount, add a comment if you like, and press Add movement.

If something does not fit, for example an account that cannot be used with that base movement, Pledger explains the problem and nothing is saved.

🩹 Fix a mistake

Movements cannot be edited. Delete the wrong one with the × button at the end of its row and add it again with the right details.

➕ Add an account

Go to Accounts, fill in the New account form (name, type and description) and press Add account.

Optionally, pick a check on its balance at the end of each day: Net positive (never negative) or Net zero (always 0.00, for an intermediary account). Movements that would break it are rejected, and the Checks column of the balances shows the accounts that have one.

➕ Add a result type or base movement

Go to Results, open New result type or New base movement, fill in the form and press the button. Every entry needs a short name and a description.

  • A new base movement that is a gain or a loss also needs a result type.
  • For a base movement's debit and credit, choose an account to always use that account, or leave - to choose the account each time.
  • A new result type is added at the end of the list.

To rename, reorder or remove entries, the settings files have to be edited by hand.

📈 Buy or sell an investment

Go to Investments and use its form, not the Ledger one. Name the investment and, when buying, selling or splitting, its quantity. The money comes from (or goes to) another account, such as your bank account. Interest, dividends and broker fees are recorded there too.

🤝 Keep track of who owes you

Use a debt account for the movement and write the person's, service's or company's name in the comment, such as "Lunch with Dad". The Debt tab adds it to them, and warns you about movements that name no one.

🔎 Look at a single month or account

Use the Month filter on Ledger, Accounts, Results, Debt or Credit Cards (it is remembered across tabs), and the Account filter on Ledger. Pick All time to go back.

Balance
How much an account holds (assets) or how much is owed on it (liabilities) at a point in time, after adding up all its movements.
Base currency
The currency every amount in Pledger is written in.
CPI (price index)
A number that follows how prices rise in your base currency, built day by day from the monthly inflation. It starts at 100.
Cumulative
In the result table of the Results tab, the net results added up from the first result type down to that row. The last row equals your profit.
Equity
Your net worth: assets minus liabilities. Gains raise it and losses lower it.
Fixed side
A debit or credit account that a base movement always uses.
Gains, losses and profit
The total of your gain movements, the total of your loss movements, and the difference between them, for the chosen period.
Initial movement
The movement that sets an account's starting amount. Pledger creates it on its own, so you never add it.
Liquid
Money you can use right away, such as cash or a checking account, unlike a fixed-term deposit.
Movement
One entry in the ledger: money moving from one account to another on a date.
Net
Gains minus losses, here for a single result type.
Price
What one unit of an investment cost on average: its amount divided by its quantity.
Real value
A value with inflation taken out, in today's prices, so you can compare amounts from different times fairly.

🤔 Why does my profit differ from the change in my bank balance?

Profit only counts gains and losses. Transfers, such as moving money to savings or paying a credit card bill, change account balances but not your profit.

🔒 Why can't I choose the equity account?

Pledger uses equity on its own: it is the credit side of every gain and the debit side of every loss, and transfers never touch it.

⚠️ The page says it could not load the ledger.

One of the settings files or the ledger has a problem, for example a movement that uses an account or base movement that no longer exists. The message says what is wrong; fix that entry in the file and reload the page.
