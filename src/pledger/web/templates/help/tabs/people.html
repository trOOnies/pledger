The people you owe and who owe you. The services and the companies have accounts of their own, in the Services and Work tabs.

🏦 Debt and loan accounts

Each currency has two of them, set in the currencies file:

  • the debt account: the debts, what you owe;
  • the loan account: the loans, what you are owed.

The tab shows what you owe each person in each account, in its currency, and in all, what they owe you, and your net debt, in the base currency.

💬 Who a movement is about

A movement of a debt account counts for whoever is named in its comment, such as "Lunch with Dad". Whoever is expected to be a creditor is only in the debt accounts, whoever is expected to be a debtor only in the loan accounts, and the rest in all of them.

A warning shows up when a movement names no one, or someone who isn't in its account, or when someone owes the other way around from what is expected.

🤝 Lend, borrow and pay back

Borrowing (Debt, then Incurred or Paid, in a debt account) and lending (Loan, then Given or Collected, in a loan account) are added, edited and deleted here, in any currency, picking who of that account they are about. The Ledger tab shows them with a 🟡.

  • Between two currencies, you also say what it was in the currency of the debt account, as it was converted when you made it (e.g. paying a debt in dollars with pesos). The accounts in other currencies are revalued with their currency, like in the Currencies tab.
  • The Who filter shows the movements of one of them, and each movement shows what you owe whoever it is about right after it (negative and green: what they owe you).

⚖️ Set remaining as Difference

With it, a new movement also settles what would remain with them in its account, showing it before you add it: a Difference (G) if you owed them, a Difference (L) if they owed you, leaving them at 0.00. It can't be edited, only deleted, here or in the Ledger tab.

➕ New person

The New person form adds someone to the creditors file, with what was owed when the ledger starts (positive if you owe them, negative if they owe you) and, optionally, their expected role.
