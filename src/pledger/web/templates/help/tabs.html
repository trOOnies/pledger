📋 Ledger
The home page: click the pledger logo to get here. Your movements, newest first, with the form to add new ones: pick its type (gain, loss or transfer) and then its base movement. Conversions, debt movements and card payments are added in their own tabs. Use the Month, Account and Base movement filters to narrow everything down (a filter in use is highlighted). Each movement shows the balance of its debit and credit accounts right after it. The movements setting each account's initial amount are left out unless you tick Initial movements. Each movement has an ID that never changes, given by pledger itself (rows added to the CSVs by hand get theirs when pledger starts). Click ✎ on a movement to edit it in the form, or × to delete it. Movements handled in another tab can't be edited or deleted here: instead, they show a colored circle linking to their row in that tab (or to the row they are generated from, like the month of a salary), highlighted: 🟢 Investments, 🔵 Currencies (conversions and revaluations), 🟠 Work (salaries), 🟣 Accounts (initial amounts, and the balance corrections of the Audit tab, which can be deleted here), 🟡 Debt (debts and loans) and Services (what the services are owed) 🔴 Credit Cards (card payments) and 🟤 Cart (the products of a cart). Their ID links there too, and the IDs of the Investments, Currencies, Debt and Credit Cards tabs link back to the same movement in the Ledger tab.
🏦 Accounts
Every account with its description and balance, your total assets, liabilities and equity, and the form to add a new account. With a month picked, balances show where each account stood at the end of that month. It also shows what your assets and liabilities are made of, by currency or by liquidity, and, at each month end, your debt over equity and your net liquidity (liquid assets minus liquid liabilities) over equity. Click ✎ on an asset or liability account to edit it (all but its name, type and currency).
💱 Currencies
Your currencies: the inflation month by month of the base currency and the price index built from it, day by day (the months that haven't ended yet are projected, and so are the estimated ones), and, for each other currency, what it is worth in the base one over time next to its real value (that value with inflation taken out, in today's prices: if it goes down, the currency grew less than prices). It also shows the balances of the accounts in each currency, what the accounts in other currencies hold in their own currency, and how your assets are split by currency at each month end. Buying or selling another currency is a conversion: add it here, picking the currency sold and the one bought, with the amount in each one (between two other currencies, it goes through the intermediary account Fi); the Currency and Account filters narrow them down, Initial movements adds the initial amounts of those accounts, and each one shows the account's balance in its own currency right after it. Every time the currency's value changes, its accounts get a Foreign currency (G) or (L) gain or loss, so their balance always matches what they hold at that value.
📊 Results
How much your equity changed in the period, nominal and real (taking out inflation), and how many months of expenses it covers (at the average monthly losses of the year up to then, leaving out the deductions of the salary). Your gains and losses grouped by result type (click a result type to see what is inside it, and tick Cumulative to add each one to the ones before it), a waterfall of the gains and then the losses of each result type adding up to your profit (with its table one click away; tick % of Operative gains to see it all as a share of the gains of your first result type), your equity in months of expenses at each month end (tick or untick the loss result types that count as expenses, or tick Raw monthly averages to see their average monthly losses stacked instead), and a line showing your equity over time, nominal and real (dashed, deflated by the CPI to the prices of its last date). Hover over the line to see each day. Initial movements are left out of the results here too, unless you tick Initial movements. Below them are your result types and base movements; you can add new ones here.
💼 Work
Your work history, month by month: the company and job position, the gross salary and its deductions (taxes, contributions... each a column of the work CSV, named after its loss base movement), which take it to the net salary, and the date you received the payment. A table shows how this year's gross salary gets to the net one. Months without a job are kept too, so no month is ever missing. Add the next month with the form (Fill in from copies the last month into it), or click ✎ on a month to edit it. Two charts show your gross and net salary over time, nominal and real (dashed). Each month's gross salary also goes into the ledger week by week, split equally between the weeks of the month (Monday to Sunday, each in the month with at least 4 of its days), and so do its deductions, out of the account the salary goes to. The Ledger tab shows these with a 🟠: change them by editing the month here.
📈 Investments
What you hold in your investment accounts: each investment with its type, quantity, amount, price (amount over quantity) and broker fees over its amount, and what your investments are made of, by type or by account. The investment results (capital gains and losses, interest, dividends, broker fees and stock splits) and the buying and selling of investments are added, edited and deleted here, not in the Ledger tab, which shows them with a 🟢. Each one names its investment and, when buying, selling or splitting, its quantity, so the amounts and quantities above stay up to date. The interest on money earned or paid in a regular account with an interest frequency (set in the Accounts tab; picking an Interest on Money base movement, with no investment) gets a section of its own, by account and month by month, and counts towards the total result. It warns about each past week without interest of a weekly account, or each past month of a monthly one. Two charts show the total invested (nominal and real, dashed) and the total result month by month. The Investment account, Investment and Base movement filters narrow the movements down (an investment shows all its movements, broker fees and dividends included), and each movement shows the total of its account and the amount and quantity of its investment right after it.
🤝 Debt
Who is in your debt accounts, two per currency (set in the currencies file): the debt account, the debts (what you owe), and the loan account, the loans (what you are owed), split into people, services and companies: what you owe each of them in each account, in its currency, and in all, what they owe you, and your net debt, in the base currency. A movement of a debt account counts for whoever is named in its comment; the companies you worked for (from the Work tab) are listed automatically. Whoever is expected to be a creditor (by default, the services) is only in the debt accounts, whoever is expected to be a debtor (by default, the companies) only in the loan accounts, and the rest in all of them. A warning shows up when a movement names no one, or someone who isn't in its account, or when someone owes the other way around from what is expected. The movements of the debt accounts are listed too, with who each one names. Borrowing (Debt, then Incurred or Paid, in a debt account) and lending (Loan, then Given or Collected, in a loan account) are added, edited and deleted here, in any currency, picking who of that account they are about; between two currencies, you also say what it was in the currency of the debt account, as it was converted when you made it (e.g. paying a debt in dollars with pesos), and the accounts in other currencies are revalued with their currency, like in the Currencies tab; the Who filter shows the movements of one of them, and each movement shows what you owe whoever it is about right after it (negative and green: what they owe you). With Set remaining as Difference, a new movement also settles what would remain with them in its account, showing it before you add it: a Difference (G) if you owed them, a Difference (L) if they owed you, leaving them at 0.00. It can't be edited, only deleted, here or in the Ledger tab.
🧾 Services
The monthly values of the services of your debt accounts (rent, gas, internet…), a column each in the services file, set month by month and split between the weeks of the month, on their Mondays (prorated, like the salaries). Each one becomes movements of its base_movement, an expense, crediting the debt account of the base currency (or its loan account, for a service expected to be a debtor: one that charges in advance), naming the service, up to today, so the Debt tab shows what you owe it; pay it from there with Debt - Paid (or, in advance, Loan - Given), or from the New service payment form of this tab: the value of the month, if known, fills in the amount (paying another one leaves the difference with the service, to balance later); if it isn't known, the amount paid can become the value of the month.
💳 Credit Cards
What you owe on each credit card, plus what was charged to it and paid off it. With a month picked, the balance is the one at the end of that month, and the charges and payments are the ones of that month. A bar chart shows what was charged each month (your purchases, not your payments), another one what is paid each month, with the purchases in installments split into them, the ones still due striped, and another one shows what you owe on them as a share of your equity, month by month, and their movements are listed below, each with what you owe on its card right after it (the Credit card filter shows the ones of one card), followed by the installments of the charges, picked by the month of their statement rather than by the date of the charge. Card payments are added, edited and deleted here, picking the card and the account paying it. A card in another currency also shows what you owe in that currency, and its payments always say how much was paid in the card's currency, whatever currency you paid with. The statement closing dates are set here in a table with a row for each month of a year and a column for each card; the months without one are autocompleted from the last one set before (the same day, as many months later), or are the last day of the month. The charts of what is charged and paid each month follow them: a charge counts in the first statement closing on or after its date, and its installments in the statements after it, still due until each one closes (the results still count it in its own month).
🛒 Cart
Enter a whole purchase at once: a cart has a date, a store and the account it is paid from (in your base currency; a credit card too), and its products go in bags. Each bag has a loss base movement and, paid with a credit card, a number of installments, shared by its products; two bags may have the same ones, as bags only organize the cart. Each product is a movement of the ledger, its comment naming the store, then the product. The carts are listed here, and are edited (keeping the IDs of the products kept) and deleted, with their movements, only from this tab.
🎯 Budget
Set a monthly budget for any loss base movement and compare it with what was spent in the month you pick, with what is left and what is over. A budget holds from its month on, until you change it, so past months keep theirs. Saving goals track the balance of an asset account against a target in its currency, with what to save each month to reach it by its due date. Nothing here adds movements to the ledger.
✈️ Trip
Plan a trip with its expenses, by category and in any currency, seen in your base currency at today's values, per day and against what you spent: the movements of the base movement you pick during the trip, listed there. As the ledger can date movements on the Monday of their week, every week it touches counts. Nothing here adds movements to the ledger.
🔍 Audit
Check your accounts against reality: fill in the balances you see in your bank or wallet without seeing pledger's, grouped into assets, investment accounts, debt, credit cards and other liabilities (all confirmed at once). Leave an account empty to skip it; a 0.00 is audited as zero. The net zero accounts, like Fi, are fixed at 0.00, and a net positive account can't be filled in as negative. Once you confirm, each balance is shown next to the actual one, and each difference offers a button to add a Correction gain or loss dated today. It's optional: you can fix the difference yourself instead. Every audit you confirm is kept in the History, with its amounts and the corrections you accepted.
