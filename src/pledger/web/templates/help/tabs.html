📋 Ledger
Your movements, newest first, with the form to add new ones: pick its type (gain, loss or transfer) and then its base movement. Conversions, debt movements and card payments are added in their own tabs. Use the Month, Account and Base movement filters to narrow everything down (a filter in use is highlighted). Each movement shows the balance of its debit and credit accounts right after it. The movements setting each account's initial amount are left out unless you tick Initial movements. Each movement has an ID that never changes, given by pledger itself (rows added to the CSVs by hand get theirs when pledger starts). Click ✎ on a movement to edit it in the form, or × to delete it. Movements handled in another tab can't be edited or deleted here: instead, they show a colored circle linking to their row in that tab (or to the row they are generated from, like the month of a salary), highlighted: 🟢 Investments, 🔵 Currencies (conversions and revaluations), 🟠 Work (salaries), 🟣 Accounts (initial amounts and balance corrections, which can be deleted here), 🟡 Debt (debts and loans) and Services (what the services are owed) and 🔴 Credit Cards (card payments). Their ID links there too, and the IDs of the Investments, Currencies, Debt and Credit Cards tabs link back to the same movement in the Ledger tab.
🏦 Accounts
Every account with its description and balance, your total assets, liabilities and equity, and the form to add a new account. With a month picked, balances show where each account stood at the end of that month. It also shows what your assets and liabilities are made of, by currency or by liquidity, and, at each month end, your debt over equity and your net liquidity (liquid assets minus liquid liabilities) over equity. Click ✎ on an asset or liability account to edit it (all but its name, type and currency), or ⚖ to correct its balance to the one you checked by hand, on a date (today by default): the difference becomes a Correction gain or loss on that date.
💱 Currencies
Your currencies: the inflation month by month of the base currency and the price index built from it, day by day (the months that haven't ended yet are projected), and, for each other currency, what it is worth in the base one over time next to its real value (that value with inflation taken out, in today's prices: if it goes down, the currency grew less than prices). It also shows the balances of the accounts in each currency, what the accounts in other currencies hold in their own currency, and how your assets are split by currency at each month end. Buying or selling another currency is a conversion: add it here, picking the currency sold and the one bought, with the amount in each one (between two other currencies, it goes through the intermediary account Fi); the Currency and Account filters narrow them down, and each one shows the account's balance in its own currency right after it. Every time the currency's value changes, its accounts get a Foreign currency (G) or (L) gain or loss, so their balance always matches what they hold at that value.
📊 Results
How much your equity changed in the period, nominal and real (taking out inflation), and how many months of expenses it covers (at the average monthly losses of the year up to then, leaving out the deductions of the salary). Your gains and losses grouped by result type (click a result type to see what is inside it, and tick Cumulative to add each one to the ones before it), a waterfall of the gains and then the losses of each result type adding up to your profit (with its table one click away; tick % of Operative gains to see it all as a share of the gains of your first result type), and a line showing your equity over time, nominal and real (dashed, deflated by the CPI to the prices of its last date). Hover over the line to see each day. Initial movements are left out of the results here too, unless you tick Initial movements. Below them are your result types and base movements; you can add new ones here.
💼 Work
Your work history, month by month: the company and job position, the gross salary and its deductions (taxes, contributions... each a column of the work CSV, named after its loss base movement), which take it to the net salary, and the date you received the payment. A table shows how this year's gross salary gets to the net one. Months without a job are kept too, so no month is ever missing. Add the next month with the form (Fill in from copies the last month into it), or click ✎ on a month to edit it. Two charts show your gross and net salary over time, nominal and real (dashed). Each month's gross salary also goes into the ledger week by week, split equally between the weeks of the month (Monday to Sunday, each in the month with at least 4 of its days), and so do its deductions, out of the account the salary goes to. The Ledger tab shows these with a 🟠: change them by editing the month here.
📈 Investments
What you hold in your investment accounts: each investment with its type, quantity, amount, price (amount over quantity) and broker fees over its amount, and what your investments are made of, by type or by account. The investment results (capital gains and losses, interest, dividends, broker fees and stock splits) and the buying and selling of investments are added, edited and deleted here, not in the Ledger tab, which shows them with a 🟢. Each one names its investment and, when buying, selling or splitting, its quantity, so the amounts and quantities above stay up to date. The interest on money earned or paid in a regular account (picking an Interest on Money base movement, with no investment) gets a section of its own, by account and month by month, and counts towards the total result. Two charts show the total invested (nominal and real, dashed) and the total result month by month. The Investment account, Investment and Base movement filters narrow the movements down (an investment shows all its movements, broker fees and dividends included), and each movement shows the total of its account and the amount and quantity of its investment right after it.
🤝 Debt
Who is in your debt account (De), split into people, services and companies: what you owe each of them, what they owe you, and your net debt. A movement of a debt account counts for whoever is named in its comment; the companies you worked for (from the Work tab) are listed automatically. A warning shows up when a movement names no one, or when someone owes the other way around from what is expected (by default, you owe services and companies owe you). The movements of De are listed too, with who each one names. Borrowing (Debt, then Incurred or Paid) and lending (Loan, then Given or Collected) are added, edited and deleted here, picking who they are about; the Who filter shows the movements of one of them, and each movement shows what you owe whoever it is about right after it (negative and green: what they owe you).
🧾 Services
The monthly values of the services of your debt account (rent, gas, internet…), a column each in the services file, set month by month and split between the weeks of the month, on their Mondays (prorated, like the salaries). Each one becomes movements of its base_movement, an expense, crediting De, naming the service, up to today, so the Debt tab shows what you owe it; pay it from there with Debt - Paid.
💳 Credit Cards
What you owe on each credit card, plus what was charged to it and paid off it. With a month picked, the balance is the one at the end of that month, and the charges and payments are the ones of that month. A bar chart shows what was charged each month (your purchases, not your payments), and another one shows what you owe on them as a share of your equity, month by month, and their movements are listed below, each with what you owe on its card right after it (the Credit card filter shows the ones of one card). Card payments are added, edited and deleted here, picking the card and the account paying it.
